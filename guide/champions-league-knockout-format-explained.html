Champions League Knockout Stage Explained

Once the 36-team league phase ends in late January, the Champions League switches to a familiar two-legged knockout — but with an extra round, and a seeding system that makes your final league position matter long after the table is finished.

The knockout play-off round

The eight clubs finishing 1st–8th skip this round entirely and go straight to the round of 16. The 16 clubs finishing 9th–24th contest a two-legged play-off in February for the remaining eight round-of-16 places.

The pairings are seeded by league-phase position. Clubs finishing 9th–16th are seeded and face a club that finished 17th–24th. Seeded clubs play the second leg at home — the advantage of finishing the tie on your own ground, with the tie's decisive moments in front of your own crowd.

Clubs eliminated in the play-off round are out of Europe altogether. There is no transfer to the Europa League at any stage of the Champions League knockouts.

Round of 16 and beyond

The eight league-phase qualifiers meet the eight play-off winners in the round of 16. From there it is a standard bracket: round of 16, quarter-finals and semi-finals are all two-legged home and away, and the final is a single match at a neutral venue selected years in advance.

League-phase position continues to apply. The clubs that finished 1st–8th are seeded in the round of 16 and again play the second leg at home. They also cannot face each other in that round, which keeps the highest-ranked sides apart for longer.

The bracket is fixed in advance

Unlike the old format, where each round was drawn fresh, the knockout bracket is determined once and then followed through to the final. The path from the round of 16 onwards is therefore known as soon as the play-offs are complete — a club can see its potential quarter-final and semi-final opponents immediately.

This is a meaningful change. It rewards finishing high in the league phase twice over: once with a seeding advantage, and again with a bracket position that keeps the other strongest clubs on the opposite side for as long as possible.

How extra time and penalties work

Two-legged ties are decided on aggregate score across both matches. If the aggregate is level after the second leg, the tie goes to extra time, and then to a penalty shoot-out if still level.

Away goals do not break ties. UEFA abolished the away-goals rule in 2021, so a 2–1 away win followed by a 1–0 home defeat is simply 2–2 on aggregate and proceeds to extra time. The final, being a single match, goes straight to extra time and penalties if level after 90 minutes.

Why the top eight matters so much

The difference between eighth and ninth is the largest single step in the competition. Eighth place means:

  • Two fewer matches — no February play-off, in a month already crowded with domestic fixtures.
  • A seeded round-of-16 tie, with the second leg at home.
  • No risk of a February exit before the round of 16 has even started.
  • A kinder bracket position, kept away from the other top seeds.
  • Roughly a month of extra recovery at the sharp end of the domestic season.

That is exactly why our simulator marks the top-eight line so prominently, and why the qualification scenarios page computes the precise number of points each club still needs to reach it.

The knockout calendar

  • Knockout play-off round: February, two legs.
  • Round of 16: March, two legs.
  • Quarter-finals: April, two legs.
  • Semi-finals: late April to early May, two legs.
  • Final: late May or early June, one match at a neutral venue.

Frequently asked questions

Which teams skip the Champions League knockout play-off round?

The eight clubs finishing 1st to 8th in the league phase go directly to the round of 16. Clubs finishing 9th to 24th contest the two-legged play-off round in February.

Do away goals count in the Champions League knockout stage?

No. UEFA abolished the away-goals rule in 2021. If a two-legged tie is level on aggregate after the second leg, it goes to extra time and then a penalty shoot-out.

Is the Champions League knockout bracket drawn round by round?

No. The bracket is determined once and followed through to the final, so clubs can see their potential quarter-final and semi-final opponents as soon as the play-off round is complete.

What happens to teams knocked out in the Champions League play-off round?

They are eliminated from European competition for that season. There is no transfer into the Europa League at any stage of the Champions League knockouts.

Which team plays the second leg at home in the knockout rounds?

The seeded club, determined by league-phase position. Clubs finishing 9th to 16th are seeded in the play-off round, and clubs finishing 1st to 8th are seeded in the round of 16.
