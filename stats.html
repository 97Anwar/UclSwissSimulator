How to read these numbers

Best attack and best defence count goals scored and conceded across league-phase matches only. They are raw totals, not per-game averages, so early in the phase a club that has played its fixtures against weaker opposition can lead on volume alone. Read them alongside the table rather than on their own.

Goal difference is not just a curiosity here. In the 36-team league phase it is the first tiebreaker, ahead of goals scored and away goals, and head-to-head records are not used at all. A club leading the goals-scored chart may therefore be several places higher in the real table than its points alone suggest. The full order is in our tiebreakers guide.

Clean sheets count matches in which a club conceded nothing. With only eight fixtures per club, a single clean sheet is worth proportionally far more than across a 38-game domestic league — which is why the leaderboard tends to move sharply after every matchday.

The winning streak counts consecutive league-phase victories that are still running. It resets on a draw or defeat, and ignores domestic results, so a club in excellent league form at home may not appear here at all.

Biggest win margin and highest-scoring match are different measures and often different fixtures. A 4–0 has a larger margin; a 3–4 has more goals. Both are listed with the matchday they occurred on so you can trace them back.

Where these figures come from

Every number on this page is computed from real, official results only. Predictions entered in the simulator are stored in your own browser and never affect these totals — if they did, the page would describe your guesses rather than the competition.

Matches that have not been played are excluded entirely rather than counted as 0–0, which is why the averages stay meaningful mid-phase. Results sync automatically from public match data on a schedule tied to the matchday calendar, so figures can briefly trail a live scoreline. Full detail is in our methodology.
